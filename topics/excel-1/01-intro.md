Microsoft Excel is a powerful computer application called a **spreadsheet**. Spreadsheets are used to organize data into rows and columns, perform calculations, and create charts.

Whether you want to track a monthly budget, create an address list, or calculate class grades, Excel makes it easy to work with numbers.

---

## The Excel Interface

When you open Excel, you'll see a screen filled with a grid of boxes. Let's look at the key parts of the screen:

### 1. Columns, Rows, and Cells
- **Columns** run vertically (up and down) and are labeled with **Letters** (A, B, C, D, etc.).
- **Rows** run horizontally (left to right) and are labeled with **Numbers** (1, 2, 3, 4, etc.).
- A **Cell** is the intersection of a column and a row. It is a single box in the grid.
- Every cell has an address or name based on its Column Letter and Row Number. For example, the very first cell in the top-left corner is **A1**.

### 2. The Active Cell
When you click on a cell, a dark green border appears around it. This is the **Active Cell**. Any text or number you type will go directly into the active cell.

### 3. The Ribbon
The Ribbon is the collection of buttons and tabs at the top of the Excel window. This is where you find tools for formatting text, changing fonts, saving files, and inserting tables.

## Practice Checklist

To get comfortable with the Excel window, open a blank Excel sheet and try the following:
1. Click on cell **B3**. Look at the top-left box (the Name Box) to verify it shows "B3".
2. Click on cell **D10**.
3. Use the arrow keys on your keyboard to move the green active cell box around.
