<h3>Part 1: Three Things That Are Not the Same</h3>

<div class="lead-in-card">
  <span class="lead-in-badge">SIMPLE CONCEPTS</span>
  <p>People often mix up regular computer programs, automated actions, and AI. Here is how to tell them apart using simple everyday examples.</p>
</div>

<p>**1. Regular Software (Computer Programs):** Think of regular software like a baking recipe written on a card. The computer follows step 1, step 2, and step 3 exactly as written. An example is a simple calculator adding up numbers.</p>

<p>**2. Automation (Automatic Triggers):** Think of automation like an automatic lawn sprinkler. It turns on at 8:00 AM every morning automatically. But it cannot tell if it is raining unless someone adds a special sensor. It just follows a simple set rule.</p>

<p>**3. Artificial Intelligence (AI):** Think of AI like an experienced worker who has looked at thousands of store receipts over many years. The worker starts to notice patterns, like people buying hot dog buns whenever they buy charcoal. **AI learns patterns from examples instead of following rigid rules.**</p>

| Type of Tool | How It Works | Everyday Analogy | Workplace Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Regular Software | Follows exact steps written by a programmer | A baking recipe followed step-by-step | An Excel spreadsheet calculating total pay |
| Automation | Runs simple actions automatically when triggered | An automatic sprinkler on a timer | Sending an auto-reply email when a web form is filled out |
| Artificial Intelligence | Learns patterns from thousands of past examples | An experienced worker recognizing common customer requests | Drafting a helpful reply to a unique client question |

<h3>Part 2: How Artificial Intelligence Learns</h3>

<div class="lead-in-card">
  <span class="lead-in-badge">HOW IT LEARNS</span>
  <p>AI does not have a human brain or feelings. It learns by looking at giant collections of past examples.</p>
</div>

<p>Teaching an AI is like showing thousands of dog photos to a toddler. You do not explain dog anatomy. You just point to pictures and say 'dog'. After seeing enough pictures, the child learns what a dog looks like.</p>

<p>An AI model looks at millions of example documents, emails, or reports. It adjusts its internal settings until it recognizes common patterns.</p>

<p>Because AI learns from past examples, **the AI is only as good as the example data it learned from.** If the past examples had mistakes, the AI will repeat those mistakes.</p>

<h3>Part 3: What AI Does Well — and Where It Fails</h3>

<div class="lead-in-card">
  <span class="lead-in-badge">WHAT WORKS</span>
  <p>Knowing what AI cannot do is just as important as knowing what it can do.</p>
</div>

<p>AI is great at summarizing long notes, drafting message outlines, fixing grammar, and translating simple text.</p>

<p>AI is bad at real human common sense, true caring empathy, and knowing live real-time news outside its training data.</p>

<p>Keep this simple working rule in mind: **AI suggests ideas, Human checks facts, Human makes the call.**</p>

| Work Task | Good for AI? | Why? | Human Job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drafting a meeting agenda outline | Yes ✅ | AI is great at organizing written text | Check times and review topic list |
| Calculating exact employee paychecks | No ❌ | Payroll requires 100% exact math rules | Use payroll software |
| Summarizing a 20-page document | Yes ✅ | AI condenses long text quickly | Make sure key points are accurate |
| Making final medical or legal calls | No ❌ | Requires real human responsibility and judgment | A licensed human professional decides |
