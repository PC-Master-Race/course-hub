<p>Map the Landscape - Know What Each Tool Can Actually Do</p>

<p>Most people open ChatGPT for everything - writing, data questions, images, scheduling.</p>

<p>That works sometimes, but it is the equivalent of using a hammer for every job in your</p>

<p>toolbox. Once you understand the five categories of AI tools, you will know which tool to</p>

<p>reach for, why it is the right fit, and when a different tool would do the job faster or better.</p>

<p>This is one of the most practical frameworks you will take out of this course.</p>

<p>The 5 Categories of AI at Work</p>

<p>Every AI tool you will encounter in the workplace - whether it is free, built into software you</p>

<p>already use, or something your employer pays for - falls into one of these five categories.</p>

<p>Some tools span more than one category, but understanding each one on its own gives you a</p>

<p>mental map that makes every new tool easier to evaluate.</p>

<p>Category 1 - Language & Communication</p>

<p>What it does: These tools work with words. They write, rewrite, summarize, translate, draft,</p>

<p>and edit text based on your instructions. This is the most visible category - it is what most</p>

<p>people think of when they hear the word AI.</p>

<p>Common tools: ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot</p>

<p>Examples across your field:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Accounting / Bookkeeping: Draft a professional email to a client explaining why their</li>
</ul>
<p>tax filing is delayed</p>

<ul>
  <li>Real Estate: Write a compelling property listing description from your bullet -point notes</li>
</ul>
<p>English brief for staff</p>

<ul>
  <li>Floristry / Small Business: Draft thank-you notes, social media captions, or responses</li>
</ul>
<p>to online reviews</p>

<ul>
  <li>Entrepreneur / Freelancer: Write a project proposal, client brief, or invoice follow -up</li>
</ul>
<p>email in minutes</p>

<p>Key insight: The better your prompt, the better the output. Language AI does not read your</p>

<p>mind - it responds to what you give it. This is exactly why RTCF matters so much for this</p>

<p>category.</p>

<p>Category 2 - Data & Analysis</p>

<p>What it does: These tools interpret numbers, spot patterns, generate reports, and help you</p>

<p>make sense of spreadsheets or datasets that would take hours to analyze manually. You do</p>

<p>not need to be a data scientist to use them.</p>

<p>Common tools: Microsoft Copilot in Excel, Julius AI, ChatGPT (with data upload)</p>

<p>Examples across your field:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Accounting / Bookkeeping: Upload a client's expense spreadsheet and ask: 'What are</li>
</ul>
<p>the top 5 spending categories this quarter and how do they compare to last quarter?'</p>

<ul>
  <li>Real Estate: Analyze a neighborhood sales history spreadsheet to identify price trends</li>
</ul>
<p>and list-to-sale ratios</p>

<ul>
  <li>Business Owner / Entrepreneur: Paste your monthly revenue data and ask for a plain -</li>
</ul>
<p>English summary of your growth pattern</p>

<ul>
  <li>Education / HR: Summarize survey responses or student performance data into key</li>
</ul>
<p>findings and recommended actions</p>

<p>Key insight: Data AI does not replace your judgment - it surfaces the patterns so you can</p>

<p>make faster, better-informed decisions. You still decide what the numbers mean for your</p>

<p>business or client.</p>

<p>Category 3 - Image & Visual</p>

<p>What it does: These tools generate, edit, and design visual content from text descriptions or</p>

<p>uploaded images. You describe what you want and the tool produces it - no design degree</p>

<p>required. We will go much deeper on this category in Lesson 5 in this session.</p>

<p>Common tools: Canva AI, Google Gemini ( Nano-banana), Adobe Firefly - ChatGPT</p>

<p>Examples across your field:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Floristry / Events: Generate a mockup of a floral arrangement for a client presentation</li>
</ul>
<p>before you buy a single stem</p>

<ul>
  <li>Real Estate: Create professional listing graphics, neighborhood flyers, or virtual staging</li>
</ul>
<p>concepts</p>

<ul>
  <li>Business / Entrepreneur: Design a social media post, logo concept, or marketing</li>
</ul>
<p>banner without hiring a designer</p>

<ul>
  <li>Education / Training: Generate custom diagrams, worksheet visuals, or presentation</li>
</ul>
<p>graphics for your materials</p>

<p>Key insight: Image AI has real limitations around accuracy and copyright - we cover those</p>

<p>honestly in Lesson 5. For now, know that it is one of the fastest ways to produce</p>

<p>professional-looking visuals for your work without a large budget.</p>

<p>Category 4 - Audio & Voice</p>

<p>What it does: These tools transcribe spoken words into text, summarize meetings or calls,</p>

<p>generate captions, and even produce synthetic voice audio. If you spend time in meetings,</p>

<p>on calls, or producing training content, this category saves significant time.</p>

<p>Common tools: Otter.ai (transcription + summaries), OpenAI Whisper (transcription),</p>

<p>Microsoft Copilot in Teams</p>

<p>Examples across your field:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Any field with meetings: Record a client meeting, upload the audio, and get a clean</li>
</ul>
<p>summary with action items in under a minute</p>

<ul>
  <li>Real Estate: Transcribe a showing walkthrough recording into a written property notes</li>
</ul>
<p>document</p>

<ul>
  <li>Education / Training: Auto-caption your recorded instructional videos to make them</li>
</ul>
<p>accessible and searchable</p>

<ul>
  <li>Business Owner: Record your own voice memo idea and have it transcribed and</li>
</ul>
<p>cleaned up into a written brief</p>

<p>Key insight: Audio AI is one of the most underused categories by beginners - and one of</p>

<p>the highest-value ones. Transcription alone saves hours each week for anyone who attends</p>

<p>regular meetings or calls.</p>

<p>Category 5 - Workflow & Automation  (Preview)</p>

<p>What it does: These tools connect other apps and tools together to automate repetitive</p>

<p>multi-step processes - without writing code. Think of it as building a pipeline: when X</p>

<p>happens, do Y, then send Z. This is the most powerful category and also the most complex.</p>

<p>We are previewing it here and going deep in a later module.</p>

<p>Common tools: Zapier AI, Microsoft Copilot (agent mode), Make (formerly Integromat)</p>

<p>Preview examples:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Business Owner: Automatically send a follow -up email when a new form submission</li>
</ul>
<p>comes in from your website</p>

<ul>
  <li>Accounting: When a new invoice is created in QuickBooks, automatically log it to a</li>
</ul>
<p>Google Sheet and notify the client</p>

<ul>
  <li>Education / HR: When a student or employee submits a form, route it to the right</li>
</ul>
<p>person and send a confirmation automatically</p>

<p>Key insight: You do not need to touch this deeply in this session - just file the concept away. By</p>

<p>A later module, the other four categories will feel natural and you will be ready to connect them.</p>

<p class="standout-question">The Simple Version: What Is Happening When You Type a Prompt?</p>
<p>Image generation AI - like the models behind Canva AI, Gemini, and Adobe Firefly - was</p>

<p>model does not search a database of existing images. It generates a brand new image, pixel</p>

<p>The technical term is diffusion - the model starts with visual noise and gradually refines it</p>

<p>into a coherent image that matches your description. Think of it like developing a</p>

<p>photograph in a darkroom, except the photograph never existed before you asked for it.</p>

<p>produce generic images. Specific prompts produce images that actually match what you</p>

<p>need.</p>

<h3>Part 4 - Honest Limitations: When Not to Use Image AI</h3>

<p>I want to be direct with you about this. Image AI is powerful - and it has real limitations you</p>

<p>need to know before you use it professionally.</p>

<p>Accuracy issues with text,</p>

<p>hands, and faces</p>

<p>If your image needs readable text, realistic hands, or accurate faces -</p>

<p>verify everything carefully before using it. AI -generated hands often</p>

<p>have too many fingers. Text inside images is frequently garbled.</p>

<p>Always zoom in and check.</p>

<p>Bias in generated images These models were trained on internet data, which reflects real-world</p>

<p>biases. If you prompt for 'a doctor' or 'a CEO' without specifying, you</p>

<p>may get results that reflect demographic stereotypes. Be intentional in</p>

<p>your prompts about representation.</p>

<p>Copyright gray area AI-generated images exist in a legal gray area. For commercial use,</p>

<p>Adobe Firefly is the safest option because it was trained on licensed</p>

<p>content. For internal or non-commercial use, Canva AI and Gemini are</p>

<p>generally fine - but check your organization's policy.</p>

<p>Do not use for medical,</p>

<p>legal, or identity-sensitive</p>

<p>content</p>

<p>Do not generate images of real, identifiable people without their</p>

<p>consent. Do not use AI-generated medical imagery for patient</p>

<p>communication without expert review. Do not use AI-generated legal</p>

<p>documents or official-looking seals.</p>

<p>The bottom line: Image AI is a powerful tool for visual concepts, mockups, and marketing</p>

<p>content. It is not a replacement for professional photography, licensed illustration, or design</p>

<p>work that requires accuracy and accountability. Use it strategically, verify the output, and</p>

<p>always ask: would I be comfortable if my client or supervisor saw exactly how this was</p>

<p class="standout-question">made?</p>

<p>Prompting</p>

<p>recommendations or decisions.</p>
