Map the Landscape - Know What Each Tool Can Actually Do  
Why This Module Matters  
Most people open ChatGPT for everything - writing, data questions, images, scheduling. 
That works sometimes, but it is the equivalent of using a hammer for every job in your 
toolbox. Once you understand the five categories of AI tools, you will know which tool to 
reach for, why it is the right fit, and when a different tool would do the job faster or better. 
This is one of the most practical frameworks you will take out of this course.  
 
 
The 5 Categories of AI at Work  
Every AI tool you will encounter in the workplace - whether it is free, built into software you 
already use, or something your employer pays for - falls into one of these five categories. 
Some tools span more than one category, but understanding each one on its own gives you a 
mental map that makes every new tool easier to evaluate.  
  
Category 1 - Language & Communication 
What it does: These tools work with words. They write, rewrite, summarize, translate, draft, 
and edit text based on your instructions. This is the most visible category - it is what most 
people think of when they hear the word AI.  
Common tools: ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot  
Examples across your field:  
• Accounting / Bookkeeping: Draft a professional email to a client explaining why their 
tax filing is delayed 
• Real Estate: Write a compelling property listing description from your bullet -point notes 
• Education / Training: Summarize a 10-page policy document into a one -page plain-
English brief for staff 
• Floristry / Small Business: Draft thank-you notes, social media captions, or responses 
to online reviews 
• Entrepreneur / Freelancer: Write a project proposal, client brief, or invoice follow -up 
email in minutes 
Key insight: The better your prompt, the better the output. Language AI does not read your 
mind - it responds to what you give it. This is exactly why RTCF matters so much for this 
category.

Category 2 - Data & Analysis 
What it does: These tools interpret numbers, spot patterns, generate reports, and help you 
make sense of spreadsheets or datasets that would take hours to analyze manually. You do 
not need to be a data scientist to use them.  
Common tools: Microsoft Copilot in Excel, Julius AI, ChatGPT (with data upload)  
Examples across your field:  
• Accounting / Bookkeeping: Upload a client's expense spreadsheet and ask: 'What are 
the top 5 spending categories this quarter and how do they compare to last quarter?'  
• Real Estate: Analyze a neighborhood sales history spreadsheet to identify price trends 
and list-to-sale ratios 
• Business Owner / Entrepreneur: Paste your monthly revenue data and ask for a plain -
English summary of your growth pattern  
• Education / HR: Summarize survey responses or student performance data into key 
findings and recommended actions  
Key insight: Data AI does not replace your judgment - it surfaces the patterns so you can 
make faster, better-informed decisions. You still decide what the numbers mean for your 
business or client. 
 
 
Category 3 - Image & Visual 
What it does: These tools generate, edit, and design visual content from text descriptions or 
uploaded images. You describe what you want and the tool produces it - no design degree 
required. We will go much deeper on this category in Module 3 tonight.  
Common tools: Canva AI, Google Gemini ( Nano-banana), Adobe Firefly , ChatGPT 
Examples across your field:  
• Floristry / Events: Generate a mockup of a floral arrangement for a client presentation 
before you buy a single stem  
• Real Estate: Create professional listing graphics, neighborhood flyers, or virtual staging 
concepts 
• Business / Entrepreneur: Design a social media post, logo concept, or marketing 
banner without hiring a designer  
• Education / Training: Generate custom diagrams, worksheet visuals, or presentation 
graphics for your materials  
Key insight: Image AI has real limitations around accuracy and copyright - we cover those 
honestly in Module 3. For now, know that it is one of the fastest ways to produce 
professional-looking visuals for your work without a large budget.

Category 4 - Audio & Voice 
What it does: These tools transcribe spoken words into text, summarize meetings or calls, 
generate captions, and even produce synthetic voice audio. If you spend time in meetings, 
on calls, or producing training content, this category saves significant time.  
Common tools: Otter.ai (transcription + summaries), OpenAI Whisper (transcription), 
Microsoft Copilot in Teams  
Examples across your field:  
• Any field with meetings: Record a client meeting, upload the audio, and get a clean 
summary with action items in under a minute  
• Real Estate: Transcribe a showing walkthrough recording into a written property notes 
document 
• Education / Training: Auto-caption your recorded instructional videos to make them 
accessible and searchable 
• Business Owner: Record your own voice memo idea and have it transcribed and 
cleaned up into a written brief  
Key insight: Audio AI is one of the most underused categories by beginners - and one of 
the highest-value ones. Transcription alone saves hours each week for anyone who attends 
regular meetings or calls.  
  
Category 5 - Workflow & Automation  (Preview)  
What it does: These tools connect other apps and tools together to automate repetitive 
multi-step processes - without writing code. Think of it as building a pipeline: when X 
happens, do Y, then send Z. This is the most powerful category and also the most complex. 
We are previewing it here and going deep in Week 3.  
Common tools: Zapier AI, Microsoft Copilot (agent mode), Make (formerly Integromat)  
Preview examples: 
• Business Owner: Automatically send a follow -up email when a new form submission 
comes in from your website  
• Accounting: When a new invoice is created in QuickBooks, automatically log it to a 
Google Sheet and notify the client  
• Education / HR: When a student or employee submits a form, route it to the right 
person and send a confirmation automatically  
Key insight: You do not need to touch this deeply tonight - just file the concept away. By 
Week 3, the other four categories will feel natural and you will be ready to connect them.

Part 3 - The Effort-to-Value Ratio  
Just because AI can do something does not mean you should ask it to. There is always a setup 
cost - writing the prompt, reviewing the output, fixing errors, formatting the result.  
  
The Key Question to Ask Before Every AI Task  
"Will the time I save on this task be greater than the time it takes me to write a good prompt, 
review the output, and correct what needs fixing?"  
If yes - delegate it to AI.  If no - do it yourself and move on.  
  
When AI Saves You Time vs. When It Does Not 
Use this as a quick gut -check: 
  
AI is likely worth it when...  AI may not be worth it when... 
 
• The task takes more than 10 minutes to do 
manually 
• You will do this same task again - weekly, 
monthly, or regularly 
• The output is a starting point you will review 
and edit, not a final product 
• The format or structure is repeatable (emails, 
summaries, reports, lists)  
 
• The task takes 2-3 minutes and requires no 
real thinking 
• It is a one-time task you will never repeat 
• The output must be perfect with zero 
tolerance for error 
• Writing the prompt and reviewing the output 
would take longer than just doing the task 
yourself 
AI is a force multiplier - not a magic shortcut. Used strategically, it gives you back hours each week. 
Used carelessly, it adds steps to tasks that were already simple.

Part 1 - How Image AI Actually Works (Plain English)  
You do not need to understand the math. You need to understand the concept well enough to 
use the tool effectively and spot its limitations.  
  
The Simple Version: What Is Happening When You Type a Prompt?  
Image generation AI - like the models behind Canva AI, Gemini, and Adobe Firefly - was 
trained on billions of images paired with text descriptions. When you type a prompt, the 
model does not search a database of existing images. It generates a brand new image, pixel 
by pixel, based on patterns it learned during training.  
The technical term is diffusion - the model starts with visual noise and gradually refines it 
into a coherent image that matches your description. Think of it like developing a 
photograph in a darkroom, except the photograph never existed before you asked for it.  
What this means for you: The better your description, the better the result. Vague prompts

Part 4 - Honest Limitations: When Not to Use Image AI  
I want to be direct with you about this. Image AI is powerful - and it has real limitations you 
need to know before you use it professionally.  
  
Limitation What This Means for Your Work 
Accuracy issues with text, 
hands, and faces 
If your image needs readable text, realistic hands, or accurate faces - 
verify everything carefully before using it. AI -generated hands often 
have too many fingers. Text inside images is frequently garbled. 
Always zoom in and check. 
Bias in generated images These models were trained on internet data, which reflects real-world 
biases. If you prompt for 'a doctor' or 'a CEO' without specifying, you 
may get results that reflect demographic stereotypes. Be intentional in 
your prompts about representation. 
Copyright gray area AI-generated images exist in a legal gray area. For commercial use, 
Adobe Firefly is the safest option because it was trained on licensed 
content. For internal or non-commercial use, Canva AI and Gemini are 
generally fine - but check your organization's policy. 
Do not use for medical, 
legal, or identity-sensitive 
content 
Do not generate images of real, identifiable people without their 
consent. Do not use AI-generated medical imagery for patient 
communication without expert review. Do not use AI-generated legal 
documents or official-looking seals.
